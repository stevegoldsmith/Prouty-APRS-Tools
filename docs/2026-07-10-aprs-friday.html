Scope

Eleven trackers were assigned, and this report covers those. Four more trackers beaconed without an assignment (testing, or left powered on) and are excluded, along with fixed infrastructure.

These maps show where each tracker's packets reached APRS-IS: anywhere an iGate heard the tracker — directly, or relayed by a digipeater — and put it on the internet. That iGate may be one of ours or any other in range; the maps are not filtered to our own stations. A gap is where no iGate heard the tracker, or it went quiet. Working out which of our digipeaters and iGates heard what is a separate analysis, not included here yet.

Window 2026-07-10 09:30 – 19:19 UTC (05:30–15:19 EDT), the span the event ran as a whole — from the first assignment going in service to net secured. It is one window for everyone, so because some assignments started later in the day and some cleared before net secured, a few tracks will still include that operator's commute to or from home.

GPS lock reporting (Voyagers)

This year we used several new Microsat Voyager trackers, which report their own GPS lock status — "GPS Lock Lost" when they lose the satellites, "GPS Locked" when they recover. That lets us say when one lost its fix and for how long. Three Voyagers ran Friday (Safety, Rover 1, Med 1); of those, only Safety reported losses inside the event window, 5 in total:

AssignmentCallsignLossesLongestNotes
SafetyW1CMS-852 minScattered through the day; two near 2 min, the rest under a minute

Safety (W1CMS-8) lost lock five times and recovered quickly each time. Its battery stayed in the normal band all day (below), so the losses are not power-related — the GPS antenna or its install on that vehicle is the thing to check. The anomaly map has the exact loss locations.
