Scope

Friday is the Prouty Ultimate — the long, mountainous northern loop, run this year in the redesigned reverse direction. 11 rovers had a Friday assignment; the rest of the fleet came out Saturday. (Four trackers beaconed Friday with no Friday role — pre-event testing or left powered on — and are excluded.) Coverage is credited to a fixed station when it digipeated a packet (CALL*) or gated it off the air (qAR/qAO); the transmitting rover's position is plotted for each, so each station's dot cloud is its real reception footprint.

GPS lock reporting (Voyagers)

The W1CMS rovers carry Microsat Voyager units that announce their own GPS state — "GPS Lock Lost" on loss, "GPS Locked" on reacquire — so we can state when each went dark and for how long. Three Voyagers ran Friday and reported 10 losses:

Faults

Lead Vehicle (NA1T-8) sent null-island (0, 0) position fixes — an invalid GPS reading. Its reported 8,752 km distance and wild speeds are artefacts of the tracker jumping between 0,0 and its real position; its true track is short. This is the one Friday tracker whose own output, not coverage, was at fault.

Logistics Coord. (N0JSR-9) froze for 30 minutes (17:46–18:17) near 43.712, −72.417 while still reporting movement — a stuck position worth a look, and notable because it is not a Voyager and so cannot self-report a GPS loss. Med 1 (W1CMS-10) had a shorter 8-minute freeze mid-afternoon.

Dropouts

Every rover logged multi-minute silences. Friday's capture pipeline ran clean (the one 11-minute capture gap was Saturday), so Friday dropouts are genuine — but whether each is terrain shadow or the tracker itself is not yet separable without cross-referencing the coverage data, which is a planned step.
