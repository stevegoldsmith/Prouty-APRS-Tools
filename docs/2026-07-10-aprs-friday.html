Scope

Eleven rovers were assigned, and this report covers those. Four more trackers beaconed without an assignment (testing, or left powered on) and are excluded, along with fixed infrastructure.

"Coverage" means which of our fixed relay sites — the digipeaters and internet gateways (N1GMC-1 and -2, the W1FN sites, and WX1NH-10) — actually heard each rover. For every packet a site passed along, we plot where the rover was when it sent it; the spread of those points shows how far that site could hear, and a hole in the spread is where it could not.

GPS lock reporting (Voyagers)

The W1CMS rovers carry Microsat Voyager units that announce their own GPS state — "GPS Lock Lost" on loss, "GPS Locked" on reacquire — so we can state when each lost its fix and for how long. Three Voyagers ran Friday and reported 10 losses:

Safety (W1CMS-8) lost lock most often but recovered within seconds each time. With its battery trend below, that points to an install or antenna issue rather than terrain.

Faults

Lead Vehicle (NA1T-8) sent null-island (0, 0) position fixes — an invalid GPS reading. Its reported 8,752 km distance and impossible implied speeds are artefacts of the tracker jumping between 0,0 and its real position; its true track is short. The fault is in the tracker's own output, not coverage.

Logistics Coord. (N0JSR-9) froze for 30 minutes (17:46–18:17) near 43.712, −72.417 while still reporting movement — a stuck position worth a look, and notable because it is not a Voyager and so cannot self-report a GPS loss. Med 1 (W1CMS-10) had a shorter 8-minute freeze mid-afternoon.

Dropouts

Every rover logged multi-minute silences. Whether each is a coverage gap or the tracker going silent is not yet separated out; that needs the coverage data cross-referenced.
