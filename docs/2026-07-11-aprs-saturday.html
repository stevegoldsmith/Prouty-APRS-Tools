Scope

Twenty-three trackers were assigned, and this report covers those. Fixed infrastructure is excluded.

These maps show where each tracker's packets reached APRS-IS: anywhere an iGate heard the tracker — directly, or relayed by a digipeater — and put it on the internet. That iGate may be one of ours or any other in range; the maps are not filtered to our own stations. A gap is where no iGate heard the tracker, or it went quiet. Working out which of our digipeaters and iGates heard what is a separate analysis, not included here yet.

One capture caveat: we dropped the connection to APRS-IS for about 11 minutes (15:03–15:14 UTC). Any apparent tracker silence in that window is ours, not the tracker's.

Window 2026-07-11 09:30 – 20:10 UTC (05:30–16:10 EDT), the span the event ran as a whole — from the first assignment going in service to net secured. It is one window for everyone, so because some assignments started later in the day and some cleared before net secured, a few tracks will still include that operator's commute to or from home.

Coverage — who handled the traffic

The map above shows where trackers were heard. This one shows which of our stations did the hearing. Each colour is one of our iGates or digipeaters; a dot is a field packet that station handled off-air — either it was the iGate that put the packet on APRS-IS (credited only for packets heard by radio, qAR/qAO), or it was a used digipeater in the path. The dot is plotted at the tracker's position, so each station's dot cloud is its real reception footprint. Toggle stations on and off to see who covered which area.

Open the coverage map full-screen. Solid dots are packets a station heard first-hand; faded dots are ones it relayed after another station had already repeated them. Our sites relaying each other's beacons is left out.

One limit to read this map by: APRS-IS keeps only the first copy of each packet, so a packet heard by several of our receivers appears once, credited to whichever delivered it. This map therefore shows who delivered each packet, which is a floor on who heard it — not the full set of receivers that copied it. Measuring true overlap would need each station's own raw receive log.

GPS lock reporting (Voyagers)

This year we used several new Microsat Voyager trackers, which report their own GPS lock status — "GPS Lock Lost" when they lose the satellites, "GPS Locked" when they recover. That lets us say when one lost its fix and for how long. Five Voyagers ran Saturday; three reported losses inside the event window, 11 in total:
