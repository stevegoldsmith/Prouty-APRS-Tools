Scope

Saturday is the Bike Event — multiple supported routes over different terrain, with the full rover fleet out. 23 of the 24 rovers were active (only W1HS-13, a Friday Ultimate tracker, sat out). Coverage is credited to a fixed station when it digipeated a packet (CALL*) or gated it off the air (qAR/qAO); the transmitting rover's position is plotted for each, so each station's dot cloud is its real reception footprint.

One capture caveat: the capture pipeline itself went silent for about 11 minutes (15:03–15:14 UTC). Any apparent rover silence in that window is ours, not the tracker's.

W1CMS-11 holds the losses worth attention: four lasted 8–16 minutes and all fell in the higher-elevation northern section in the afternoon — terrain shadowing, the kind of spot where a rover looks stuck on the map while still moving. The anomaly map shows exactly where, which is the input for deciding where a fill-in digi would help next year. W1CMS-7's tight same-spot burst points at one specific shadowed location rather than a tracker problem.

Faults

W1CMS-8 froze for 16 minutes (15:22–15:38) near 43.969, −72.116 while reporting movement. NA1T-8 had a 10-minute freeze (13:35–13:45) but, unlike Friday, sent no null-island fixes — it behaved better Saturday.

Dropouts

Every rover logged multi-minute silences, scattered rather than synchronised. The one cluster around 15:03–15:14 lines up with the capture-pipeline gap above and is ours. The rest are not yet attributable to terrain versus tracker without cross-referencing the coverage data — a planned step.
